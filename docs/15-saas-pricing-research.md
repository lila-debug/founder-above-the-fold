The product is deliberately not a like-for-like scheduler. Its safer differentiators are owner-controlled profile copy, voice-to-text, manual LinkedIn profile placement, a locked publishing boundary, matching manuals, and optional local AI.

### Recommended Price Jig

**Public shelf test:** CA$199 one time (approximately US$149 as a positioning anchor, not a fixed conversion).

**Founding cohort:** CA$129 one time for the first 50 verified owners.

**Included:** current major version, web and iPhone surfaces for one owner, voice-to-text, compatible-device local AI, Perks Hub, and twelve months of product updates.

**Later major upgrade:** test CA$69. The owner may keep using the purchased major version without upgrading.

**Optional managed hosting, only if recurring cost evidence supports it:** test CA$15/month or CA$150/year. This must remain a separately chosen convenience; it must not turn the purchased app into a disguised subscription.

Why this fits the current evidence:

1. CA$199 is materially below one year of AuthoredUp and Taplio at their observed US-dollar list prices, while remaining above a commodity scheduler.
2. A one-time price needs room for support, refunds, taxes, payment fees, and a twelve-month update promise. CA$49–99 would create a fragile support equation unless the product is nearly self-serve.
3. Apple defines a non-consumable in-app purchase as bought once and non-expiring. That is the correct App Store fastener for the promise. [Apple purchase configuration](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/configure-in-app-purchase-settings/overview-for-configuring-in-app-purchases/)
4. A qualifying developer in Apple’s Small Business Program may receive a 15% commission rate up to the programme threshold; eligibility must be confirmed rather than assumed. [Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)

### Assembly Steps
#### Step 1 - Label the licence
Diagram:
```text
[CA$199]
   |
   +--> [Keep purchased major version]
   +--> [12 months updates]
   +--> [No automatic renewal]
```
Do:
1. Place the plain-language promise beside the price.
2. Label future major upgrades as optional.
3. Keep restore-purchase visible on iOS.

Check:
- The receipt restores on another device using the same Apple ID.

Avoid:
- “Lifetime updates.” That promise is uncosted and ambiguous.

#### Step 2 - Clamp the unit economics
Diagram:
```text
[Shelf price] - [store/payment fee] - [tax handling] - [refunds] - [support] = [contribution]
```
Do:
1. Record support minutes for every founding customer.
2. Reserve 15% for storefront commission only if programme eligibility is proven; model 30% as the safety case.
3. Keep hosting and cloud-model costs out of the permanent licence unless a sustainable allowance is explicit.

Check:
- At CA$199 and a 15% store commission, the pre-tax/pre-support remainder is CA$169.15. At 30%, it is CA$139.30.

Avoid:
- Calling these figures profit; support, tax, refunds and acquisition are unknown.

#### Step 3 - Run the falsification test
Diagram:
```text
[50 qualified visitors] ---> [price page] ---> [purchase / decline reason]
```
Do:
1. Show CA$129 founding and CA$199 standard pricing to a consented, relevant cohort.
2. Measure checkout starts, completed purchases, refunds, support time, and the first stated reason for declining.
3. Interview purchasers after seven days on whether the one-time promise caused trust or confusion.

Check:
- Keep CA$199 only if at least 5 of 50 qualified visitors buy or place a refundable/no-charge reservation, refunds stay below 8%, and median first-30-day support stays below 90 minutes. These are decision thresholds, not market facts.

Avoid:
- Counting unqualified social impressions as the denominator.

### Safety Stickers
- [Security] Never place a purchase receipt, email key, or secret in a visible profile panel.
- [Privacy] Explain collection, purposes, parties and meaningful risks before consent; optional collection needs a real choice. [Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/p_principle/principles/p_consent/)
- [Mobile] Ask for microphone and camera access at the physical moment of use, not during an unexplained startup sweep. [OPC mobile app guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/mobile-and-digital-devices/mobile-apps/gd_app_201210/)
- [Cookies] Cookiebot belongs on cookie-bearing web surfaces. Native iOS needs permission prompts, App Store privacy answers, and ATT only if the app tracks across other companies’ apps or sites. [Apple privacy guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/)
- [Cost] Do not promise lifetime hosting or unlimited cloud AI inside a one-time price.
- [IP] Local AI output and uploaded files still require an owner-side rights check before public use.
- [Evidence] The price is a recommended test supported by public anchors—not a proven optimum.
